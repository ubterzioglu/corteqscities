FESTIVALS

The most spectacular festival in the world

Light, colour and plenty of joy. Every year, Seville throws itself into La Feria de Abril, a microcosm where the unique character of a city unfolds with all its charm and all its power of seduction.

This year, it takes place from Tuesday 13th to Sunday 18th April.

Discover La Feria de Abril guide and get ready to enjoy the full experience: traditions, atmosphere and all the practical information you need to enjoy this unique celebration.

Feria de Abril Guide

Step into a world of sensations

La Feria de Abril begins on the night of the ‘alumbrao’, the opening ceremony when the lights of El Real are switched on. On this night, visitors also enjoy the traditional ‘pescaíto frito’ (fried fish) in the ‘casetas’ (the traditional fair tents where people gather to eat, drink, and dance). After a week of non-stop festivities, La Feria concludes with a spectacular fireworks display by the River Guadalquivir.

La Feria is like a small, festively decorated city. A fleeting world where houses are casetas, lighting is provided by farolillos, and all the streets are named after legendary bullfighters.

A city with a special access

A vibrant spectacle, every year a monumental gateway is erected at the entrance to the site (La Portada), commemorating a landmark in the city of Seville. La Portada is illuminated by thousands of light bulbs and remains a popular meeting place for the people of Seville.

Life in a week

One might say there is a unique festival calendar. There are people in Seville who experience the ‘night of the alumbrao’ as if it were New Year’s Eve. The lighting of la Portada marks, in a way, the start of another year – a new year brimming with hopes and emotions that unfold with dazzling intensity during that week. It is as if life were concentrated into a single week.
–
The streets are covered with albero, a yellowish soil from the neighbouring town of Alcalá de Guadaira. This is the sand traditionally used in Seville’s gardens and bullrings.
–
La Feria is lit up along its streets with thousands of light bulbs covered by ‘farolillos’ (a sort of spherical screen made of folded paper).

To bear in mind

01_

In 1846, a Catalan and a Basque founded La Feria de Abril.

02_

El traje de flamenca (typical dress) became established in Seville following the 1929 Exhibition.

03_

Manzanilla is the wine of la Feria; 1.5 million half-bottles are consumed.

04_

The unique style of the casetas was designed by the painter Gustavo Bacarisas.

05_

La Feria de Abril covers an area of 450,000 square metres and features 1,052 casetas.

Casetas

The casetas (the traditional fair tents where people gather to eat, drink, and dance) are one of the defining symbols of La Feria and the place where much of its distinctive atmosphere is experienced. Decorated in their own unique style, they bring together food, music, dancing and socialising throughout the week.

Although many casetas are private and belong to families, groups or associations, La Feria also has public casetas open to visitors and sevillians, designed for enjoying the festivities and experiencing one of its most representative traditions at first hand.

Caseta de Sevilla

Seville Tourist Board has a public caseta located at 225–229 Pascual Márquez Street, a space designed for enjoying La Feria in a traditional setting.

La caseta features an indoor area and a terrace, and advance booking is required for tables. Furthermore, during set times, it offers live music featuring sevillanas and rumbas, allowing you to experience la Feria in all its character.

A more affordable option at La Feria

El Real feature accessible routes with adapted sidewalks and crossings that facilitate movement for those in wheelchairs or with reduced mobility.

As a new feature, a new pedestrian access point has been added, connecting Pepe Luis Vázquez Street with Juan Pablo II Avenue, improving the flow of entry and exit and providing closer access to public transport services such as taxis and buses.

Additionally, La Portada de La Feria, one of the main entrance points to El Real, features adapted access points.

Dedicated parking and accessible transport

In the vicinity of El Real, reserved parking spaces are available for People with Reduced Mobility (PRM), located on Maestranza Aérea Avenue and Virgen de la Cinta Street. To use these spaces, it is necessary to have a valid Parking Card for People with Reduced Mobility.

The taxi ranks and TUSSAM bus stops near la Feria are equipped to facilitate the boarding and alighting of people with disabilities or reduced mobility.

Attractions with quiet hours

During La Feria de Abril 2026, Calle del Infierno will introduce quiet hours for people with hearing sensitivity on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Fridays. On these days, music and sound effects from the attractions will be turned down or switched off between 4 pm and 7 pm.

Accessible toilets and public toilets

There are adapted public restroom modules distributed at various strategic points, the locations of which can be found on the official accessibility map.

Although most of the casetas are private, la Feria also offers public casetas with free access, which typically provide better technical accessibility conditions.

Special Feria buses

The bus is one of the best ways to get to El Real, as the service is accessible to people with reduced mobility.
