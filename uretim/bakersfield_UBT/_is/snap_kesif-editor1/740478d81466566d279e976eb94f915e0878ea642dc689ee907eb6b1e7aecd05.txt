Buck Owens’ Crystal Palace closes its doors after nearly 30 years
Robert Price
1 year ago
BAKERSFIELD, Calif. (KGET) — Buck Owens’ Crystal Palace has permanently closed its doors. That’s the word from the Buck Owens family, which made the announcement Monday morning.
The museum, concert venue and dinner theater, which the late country music legend opened in 1996, held its final event last weekend, a two-night Buck Owens Birthday Bash.
“For over 28 years we brought the best of local and national entertainment to Bakersfield, served great food, and hosted thousands of special occasions for thousands of special people and families,” the family said in a statement. “Although Buck left us in 2006, we continued to maintain the excellence that he expected, striving to carry out his dream. Now, a challenging business climate plus the wishes of the Owens family members to step back from the responsibilities of management require finally shutting the doors.”
The family statement expressed hope that new owners will step forward. Meanwhile, the statement said, the family will do its best to fulfill scheduled commitments. That apparently includes Guitar Masters’ Sept. 11 show featuring Jake Shimabukuro. However, dinner and drinks will be available only if an outside vendor is brought in. Those details are yet to be announced.
“We know Buck would want us to thank the people of Bakersfield and fans from around the world for all of their support over the past years. We will miss you all,” the statement concluded.
