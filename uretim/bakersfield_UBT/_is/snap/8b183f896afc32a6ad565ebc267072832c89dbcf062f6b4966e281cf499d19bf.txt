Locals remember memories of Trout's, after fire destroys it

Bakersfield, Calif. (KBAK/KBFX) —The starting point of the trailblazers of the Bakersfield sound like Buck Owens, Merle Haggard and Red Simpson up in flames.
"It's a very big loss to the Oildale community," Donna Clopton for the Oildale Community Action Team, said. "Yeah, Trout's was known for the Honky Tonk way back in the day and my husband and I spent many times there."
The Kern County Fire Department got the call for the fire Sunday night at about a quarter till' midnight.
When firefighters got there 25% of the building was already on fire.
"It's just a great loss to Bakersfield's history," Glenda Rankin , Co-Founder at Citizens Preserving History, said. We have a hard time with all of the buildings that have been torn down over the years and then just some of them were dilapidated and had to go, but Trout's there for so many years stayed as a focal point."
Anna Reading-Cary's Father Lloyd Reading played at Trout's.
"He played here at Trout's many, many times," Reading-Cary, said. "He played upstairs on stage and then they had a downstairs called "The Green Room", where all of the musicians, the Bakersfield Sound musicians would go downstairs and just jam all night long."
Di Sharman, Vice President of Citizens Preserving History says Buck Owens, Merle Haggard, Red Simpson and many others got their start playing at honky tonk places in Oildale, like Trout's.
"It gave an opportunity for all the early musicians to have a place to play," Sharman, said. "There was Trout's here, there was the Blackboard, the Lucky Spot, there was a Honky Tonk at just about every corner in Bakersfield in the early days."
Trout's closed its doors back in 2017, and was bought by Kern Medical Properties.
It was undergoing renovations to become a medical facility.
In 2018 the non-profit Citzens Preserving History got the bench from Trout's to move it to the Kern County Museum.
"We contacted the new owners of Trout's, the medical company and they allowed us to take the Trout's bench," Sharman, said.
This is apart of a Trout's exhibit at the Kern County Museum.
As for the latest into the investigation of the fire, a cause' has still not been figured out and there were no injuries.
