Kern County requires some businesses to obtain permits to conduct their operations. These requirements vary based on location, type of service provided and whether construction is involved. Additionally, Kern County requires a film permit for all projects filming within the county boundaries.

City-specific permits may also be required for businesses operating within an incorporated City. See our City Requirements page for links to the official websites of the incorporated cities in Kern County.

Building Permit

A building permit is required for any construction in unincorporated areas of Kern County which physically changes or adds structures to your property. It is also required for any work regulated by local Codes and Ordinances, such as: new buildings, room additions, patio covers, swimming pools, roof alterations, garage conversions, exterior stucco, replacements of water heaters and other plumbing fixtures, air conditioning units, new electrical service, rewiring, moving or demolishing a building, and installation of heating equipment.

For more information on when and how to apply for a building permit, visit the Kern County Public Works Department Building Inspection page.

Environmental Health Permits

Per Kern County Code of Ordinance 8.04, the Director of Public Health Services is responsible for ensuring all health and safety standards are adhered to in the following business and service activities: food, water, land, waste, and consumer protection. For more information visit the Permitting & Compliance section of the Kern County Public Health Services Department website.

Film Permit

A valid Kern County Commercial Film Permit is required for ALL PROJECTS filming within the county boundaries—including those taking place on federal, state, civic, special district or private properties. Additional permits or approvals may be required depending upon the specific location and activity. For information regarding all the permits that may be required, visit the Film Commission Permits page.

Fire Permits

For businesses operating in unincorporated areas of Kern County, certain permits from the Kern County Fire Department may be required depending on business activity. For more information on Fire Permits, see the Kern County Fire Department's Fees, Permits, Apps page.

Food Permits

Any person conducting business or activity listed in Kern County Code of Ordinance 8.04 Environmental Health Permits and Service Fees within the geographic area under the jurisdiction of the Director of Public Health Services must apply for all applicable permits.

For more information on business activities requiring an environmental health permit visit the Kern County Public Health Services Department website.
